# The four domains of emotional intelligence

**Covers:** Self-awareness, Self-management, Social awareness, Relationship management

## Two inward skills, two outward skills {#overview}
Emotional intelligence is a big idea, and it is easier to practice once you split it into parts. A common model uses four domains in a two-by-two grid. Two are about you and two are about other people. Two are about noticing and two are about acting. Each domain builds on the one before it, so the order matters. You cannot manage a feeling you have not noticed, and you cannot handle a relationship well if you misread what the other person feels. Let's take them one by one.

## Self-awareness {#self-awareness}
Self-awareness is where everything starts. It means noticing your own emotions accurately as they happen, and understanding how they affect your thoughts and actions. In practice you name the feeling, instead of being blindsided by it an hour later.

A person with this skill can say "I am irritated, and I think it is because I did not sleep", and not only "this meeting is pointless". It also covers knowing your strengths, your limits and your values, so that you neither overrate nor underrate yourself.

## Self-management {#self-management}
Once you notice a feeling, what do you do with it? Self-management is the answer: doing something useful with the emotions you notice. That might mean staying calm under pressure, pausing before you react, or following through when you feel like quitting. It is not suppression. You are not pretending to feel nothing. You are choosing your response.

It also covers handling strong feelings, staying flexible when plans change, keeping commitments and staying hopeful after setbacks. The usual first move is plain: take a breath and let the first wave pass before you speak.

## Social awareness {#social-awareness}
Now turn your attention outward. Social awareness is picking up on other people's emotions and on the mood of a group, and not only on your own feelings. It includes reading tone of voice and body language, understanding what someone needs, and sensing the unspoken rules of a situation.

It starts with attention. A person who is checking their phone while someone speaks is not collecting the information that social awareness needs.

## Relationship management {#relationship-management}
The last domain puts the other three to work. Relationship management means using your understanding of emotions, yours and other people's, to handle relationships well. It includes giving clear feedback, settling disagreements, inspiring and guiding people, and building trust over time.

This is the visible part of emotional intelligence, the one that colleagues and friends notice. But it depends on the other three. A leader who gives feedback without self-management may say something hurtful. Without social awareness, the same leader may choose a moment when the listener cannot hear it.

## A worked example {#example}
Imagine you are leading a meeting, and a colleague, Dana, criticizes your plan sharply. Let's apply the four domains in order.

| Domain | What you do |
|---|---|
| Self-awareness | Notice your heart rate rising and the thought "she is attacking me" |
| Self-management | Take a breath, wait two seconds, and keep your voice level |
| Social awareness | Notice that Dana's arms are crossed and she looks tired; she may be under pressure |
| Relationship management | Say: "Thanks for being direct. What worries you most? Let's look at it together." |

Try skipping a row to see what happens. If you skip the first, the later ones never start. If you skip the second, you may react angrily before you have understood anything. The whole sequence takes about ten seconds, and it can change the outcome of the meeting.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to work only on the outward skills, such as being friendlier or giving more praise, while ignoring the inward ones. Without self-awareness these become a performance, and others can sense it.

A second slip is thinking that self-management means hiding your feelings. Hidden feelings usually leak out later, often in worse ways.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The four domains are self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and relationship management.
- The first two are about you. The last two are about other people.
- Each domain supports the next, so practice them in order.
- Self-management means choosing a response, not hiding what you feel.
