# The four domains of emotional intelligence

**Covers:** Self-awareness, Self-management, Social awareness, Relationship management

## Two inward skills, two outward skills {#overview}
Emotional intelligence is easier to practice once it is split into parts. A common model uses four domains arranged in a two-by-two grid: two about you and two about other people, two about noticing and two about acting. Each domain builds on the one before it, so the order matters. You cannot manage a feeling you have not noticed, and you cannot handle a relationship well if you misread what the other person feels.

## Self-awareness {#self-awareness}
Self-awareness is the ability to notice your own emotions accurately as they happen, and to understand how they affect your thoughts and actions. It means naming the feeling instead of being blindsided by it an hour later.

A person with this skill can say "I am irritated, and I think it is because I did not sleep", not just "this meeting is pointless". It also includes knowing your strengths, your limits and your values, so you do not overrate or underrate yourself.

## Self-management {#self-management}
Self-management is doing something useful with the emotions you notice, such as staying calm under pressure, pausing before you react and following through when you feel like quitting. It is not suppression. You are not pretending to feel nothing; you are choosing your response.

It covers handling strong feelings, staying flexible when plans change, keeping commitments and staying hopeful after setbacks. The usual first move is simple: take a breath and let the first wave pass before you speak.

## Social awareness {#social-awareness}
Social awareness is picking up on other people's emotions and on the mood of a group, not only your own feelings. It includes reading tone of voice and body language, understanding what someone needs, and sensing the unspoken rules of a situation.

It starts with attention. A person who is checking their phone while someone speaks is not collecting the information that social awareness needs.

## Relationship management {#relationship-management}
Relationship management is using your understanding of emotions, yours and others', to handle relationships well. It includes giving clear feedback, settling disagreements, inspiring and guiding people, and building trust over time.

This is the visible part of emotional intelligence, the one that colleagues and friends notice. But it depends on the other three. A leader who gives feedback without self-management may say something hurtful, and without social awareness may choose a moment when the listener cannot hear it.

## A worked example {#example}
Imagine you are leading a meeting, and a colleague, Dana, criticizes your plan sharply. Apply the four domains in order:

| Domain | What you do |
|---|---|
| Self-awareness | Notice your heart rate rising and the thought "she is attacking me" |
| Self-management | Take a breath, wait two seconds, and keep your voice level |
| Social awareness | Notice that Dana's arms are crossed and she looks tired; she may be under pressure |
| Relationship management | Say: "Thanks for being direct. What worries you most? Let's look at it together." |

If you skip the first row, the later ones never start. If you skip the second, you may react angrily before you have understood anything. The whole sequence takes about ten seconds, and it can change the outcome of the meeting.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Working only on the outward skills, such as being friendlier or giving praise, while ignoring the inward ones. Without self-awareness these become a performance that others can sense. A second mistake is thinking that self-management means hiding feelings. Hidden feelings usually leak out later, often in worse ways.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The four domains are self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and relationship management.
- The first two are about you. The last two are about other people.
- Each domain supports the next, so practice them in order.
- Self-management means choosing a response, not hiding what you feel.
