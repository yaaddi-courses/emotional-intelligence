# What emotional intelligence is, and what it is not

**Covers:** Emotional intelligence, EQ, EI, IQ, GPA, Goleman

## A different kind of smart {#overview}
Most of us know someone who is brilliant at exams and poor at working with people, and someone else who is not a top scorer but whom everyone trusts to handle a tense moment. The difference is not luck. It is a set of skills about feelings: noticing them, understanding them and using them well. Psychologists call this emotional intelligence, and it is worth learning for the same reason you would learn any other practical skill.

## Emotional intelligence {#emotional-intelligence}
Emotional intelligence is the ability to recognize emotions, in yourself and in other people, to understand what they mean, and to use that knowledge to guide your thinking and behavior. It does not mean being nice, being emotional or hiding your feelings. A person with high emotional intelligence can be firm, direct and calm because they know what they feel and what the other person needs.

The idea was introduced in 1990 by the psychologists Peter Salovey and John Mayer. It became widely known five years later through a book on the subject.

## EQ {#eq}
EQ stands for emotional quotient, a name chosen to sit beside IQ. People use it as the everyday shortcut for emotional intelligence: "she has a high EQ". Be careful with the number idea. There is no single, universally accepted EQ test that gives a score the way an IQ test does. Some tests exist, but they measure different things and disagree with each other, so treat any EQ number as a rough indication.

## EI {#ei}
EI is the abbreviation for emotional intelligence, and researchers often prefer it to EQ because it makes no claim about a score. In books and articles the two are interchangeable. If you see EI, read it as the same skill set.

## IQ {#iq}
IQ, or intelligence quotient, is a score from tests of reasoning and problem solving. By design the average is 100, and about two-thirds of people score between 85 and 115. IQ predicts some things quite well, such as school results and performance in work that involves complex mental tasks.

It does not predict everything. Two people with the same IQ can differ a lot in how they handle stress, disagreement or a setback. That is the space that emotional skills fill.

## GPA {#gpa}
GPA, or grade point average, is a number that summarizes a student's grades across courses. It is often used as a measure of academic success. It shows how someone performed in a particular system, and it tends to reflect IQ together with effort and habits.

## Goleman {#goleman}
Daniel Goleman is the psychologist and science writer who made emotional intelligence famous with his 1995 book. He later organized it into four domains: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and relationship management. This course follows that structure.

Goleman made strong claims about how much emotional skill matters for success. Researchers have since argued that some of those claims were too large, so the sensible conclusion is that EI matters, along with other abilities, not that it matters more than everything else.

## A worked example {#example}
Two new managers, Rosa and Ken, have the same IQ and similar grades. A project falls behind.

| | Rosa | Ken |
|---|---|---|
| Notices her own reaction | "I am worried, and it is making me short with people" | Snaps at the team |
| Reads the team | Sees that two people look overwhelmed | Assumes everyone is lazy |
| Responds | Calls a short meeting, asks what is blocking people | Adds more deadlines |
| Result | The team reports problems early | The team hides problems |

Nothing in this table depends on a test score. The difference lies in four skills: noticing, reading others, responding and building trust. Each of them can be practiced, which is why the rest of the course is built on practice.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Using EQ as a label for who is "better". People vary on many abilities, and a high IQ does not cancel the need for emotional skill, nor the reverse. Another mistake is to believe that emotional skill is fixed at birth. The evidence is that it improves with training and feedback.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Emotional intelligence is recognizing, understanding and using emotions well, in yourself and others.
- EQ and EI are everyday names for it. Treat any single EQ score with caution.
- IQ and GPA measure other abilities, and emotional skill adds something they do not capture.
- Goleman popularized the four-domain model that this course follows.
